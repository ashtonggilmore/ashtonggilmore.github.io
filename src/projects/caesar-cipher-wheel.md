## A classroom tool with very little setup

The wheel lets students choose a letter shift, encode a message, and decode it again. I designed both the physical tool and a single-page guide so students could start using it with minimal explanation.

## Decisions that made it practical

- **Print in place.** A tapered fit lets the inner disc rotate without requiring assembly after printing.
- **Eight wheels per build plate.** The 100 mm diameter leaves room for readable letters while making class sets practical to produce.
- **One color change.** Black-and-white lettering stays legible without repeated material changes.
- **Optional detent.** A spring-loaded insert adds indexing at each letter position.

I tested three versions to adjust the clearance between the disc and outer ring. The guide uses large type, numbered steps, and the same straightforward visual style as the wheel.

## Trying it with students

A small group worked through the activity using the guide. They could turn the wheels easily and use them to encode and decode messages. Their feedback informed a revision to the decoding instructions.

The center also has a recessed circle for an optional engraved badge, allowing branded versions without redesigning the wheel.
